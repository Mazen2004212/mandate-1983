## Narrative Identity

Write an original alternate-history political thriller set during a fictional 1980s Cold War. Focus on government, institutions, family, war, elections, diplomacy, intelligence, and personal responsibility. Keep the experience serious but not joyless, complex without becoming incomprehensible, and independent from existing political games.

## Core Themes

- Power and responsibility.
- Institutional survival.
- Family and public office.
- Loyalty and betrayal.
- Reform and resistance.
- Memory and legacy.
- Security and freedom.
- War and diplomacy.
- Public truth and private truth.
- Political survival and moral cost.

## Narrative Perspective

Use second-person or a close presidential perspective where appropriate. Limit information to what the president can reasonably receive through institutions and people. Never reveal hidden threats omnisciently during ordinary play. Let reports be incomplete, mistaken, biased, or manipulated.

## Prose Standards

- Write clear, screen-readable paragraphs with a strong first sentence.
- Use concrete details, period-specific objects, and credible institutions.
- Control exposition, distinguish voices, minimize repetition, and keep tense consistent.
- Use correct titles, offices, dates, and forms of address.

Use these ranges as guidance rather than rigid limits:

| Content | Recommended length |
| --- | --- |
| Briefing summary | 80-180 words |
| Dialogue turn | 15-90 words |
| Major scene opening | 120-280 words |
| Choice label | 4-18 words |
| Newspaper headline | 4-12 words |
| Newspaper summary | 35-100 words |
| Intelligence summary | 80-220 words |
| Decree summary | 80-180 words |
| Epilogue segment | 80-200 words |

## Character Voice Template

Document:

- **Character:** Stable character ID and display role.
- **Public voice:** How the character addresses institutions and audiences.
- **Private voice:** How the character changes in confidence.
- **Formality:** Formal, mixed, or conversational register.
- **Vocabulary:** Repeated preferences and forbidden anachronisms.
- **Sentence rhythm:** Typical length, cadence, and interruption style.
- **Humor:** Frequency, target, and degree of restraint.
- **Emotional restraint:** What is shown, concealed, or displaced.
- **Professional language:** Field-specific concepts used naturally.
- **Political assumptions:** Values treated as obvious by the character.
- **Topics avoided:** Subjects evaded or redirected.
- **Pressure behavior:** Changes during danger, humiliation, or loss.
- **Relationship-specific changes:** Differences with the president, family, allies, and rivals.

## Scene Categories

Apply category-specific emphasis:

| Category | Primary standards |
| --- | --- |
| Cabinet | Competing portfolios, collective responsibility, visible alliances |
| Private political meeting | Leverage, trust, deniability, personal stakes |
| Family scene | Independent goals, intimacy, public-office intrusion |
| Intelligence briefing | Confidence levels, contradictions, source limits |
| Diplomatic negotiation | Recognition, leverage, treaties, credible consequences |
| Parliamentary negotiation | Procedure, blocs, amendments, vote arithmetic |
| Economic briefing | Constraints, forecasts, distribution, delayed effects |
| Military briefing | Readiness, logistics, morale, abstract strategic risk |
| War Room | Uncertainty, command responsibility, civilian and diplomatic costs |
| Coup crisis | Conflicting reports, loyalty, legitimacy, institutional response |
| Election | Regional variation, organization, integrity, public interpretation |
| Newspaper | Outlet voice, headline, facts, framing, omissions |
| Television | Visual framing, presenter voice, concise public explanation |
| Radio | Sound-led immediacy, concise attribution, period cadence |
| Law or decree | Authority, provisions, costs, supporters, opponents, actions |
| Ending | Earned state-based outcome with mixed consequences |
| Epilogue | Character-specific legacy and long-term institutional result |

## Information Quality

Use labels consistently:

- **Confirmed:** Independently established fact.
- **Highly credible:** Strong sourcing with limited remaining doubt.
- **Probable:** Best-supported assessment, not established fact.
- **Unverified:** Plausible but insufficiently supported.
- **Disputed:** Credible sources materially conflict.
- **Likely fabricated:** Evidence strongly suggests invention.
- **Deliberate disinformation:** Evidence supports intentional deception.

Make narrative wording match confidence. Never write probability as certainty.

## Political Balance

- Give conflicts competing incentives and factions internal disagreement.
- Write competent opponents and flawed allies.
- Never represent an ideology only through caricatures.
- Base consequences on conditions rather than authorial punishment.

## Family Writing

Give family members independent goals, credible disagreements, actions outside presidential control, memories of personal and political treatment, and influence on and from public events. Never reduce family scenes to romance meters.

## Romance and Scandal

Use adults only, non-explicit presentation, emotional and political meaning, and multiple interpretations where appropriate. Avoid gratuitous content and automatic affairs used solely for shock.

## War Writing

Remain at the abstract strategic level. Show human and political consequences, uncertainty, logistics, and morale. Exclude graphic gore and real-world tactical instruction.

## Ending Writing

Write earned, mixed outcomes with character-specific epilogues and state-based variation. Never use single-choice endings or reuse identical text across unrelated endings.
